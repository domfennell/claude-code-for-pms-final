# Release 4.2 — what happened to responders

**For:** Director of Product, Engineering Manager
**From:** PM, Rook Dispatch
**Status:** Draft — findings solid, a few items still open (see below)

---

## Headline

4.2 didn't just change ranking and timing — it exposed a pre-existing bug
that penalizes a missed ping the same as a refusal, with no way to
recover. It has switched off at least 4 responders entirely, with 2 more
showing a milder version of the same thing.

## The one number

Combined weekly callouts taken by the 4 worst-hit responders fell more
than 95% — from roughly 37 a week, combined, to effectively zero — and
three weeks on, there's no recovery.

## What not to lead with, and why

The company-wide acceptance rate (77% before 4.2 → 54% the week 4.2
shipped → 73% by late August) looks like it's recovering. It isn't a
fix — it's the same mechanism redistributing work to responders who were
already winning, while the worst-hit group stays at zero. Leading with
this number alone would tell the Director the opposite of what's true.

## The mechanism (for Engineering)

- 4.2 changed three values in `config.py`: proximity weight up
  (0.45→0.60), recent-acceptance weight down (0.40→0.25), ping timeout
  down (90s→60s).
- `history.py` scores a missed ping identically to a deliberate decline,
  and the score never decays back to neutral. This predates 4.2 — the
  internal glossary confirms it's documented, intended design, not an
  undiscovered bug.
- The shorter timeout generates more misses → scored as refusals →
  lowers an already-less-weighted acceptance score → lower ranking →
  fewer pings → no way to recover. A compounding loop, not three
  independent tuning choices.
- Confirmed in the data: the four worst-hit responders used to win
  almost every callout in their own home area; they now lose nearly all
  of them, even on their own turf. This rules out "they were never that
  competitive" as the explanation.

### Two responders, same release, opposite outcomes

Farlight (one of the 4 worst-hit) and Nightwell (one of the growing
responders) sat side by side on the same release, in the same week:

| Week starting | Farlight: taken | Farlight: missed | Nightwell: taken | Nightwell: missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 Jun | 9 | 0 | 11 | 1 |
| 6 Jul | 9 | 0 | 10 | 0 |
| 13 Jul | 8 | 1 | 12 | 1 |
| 20 Jul | 9 | 1 | 12 | 1 |
| 27 Jul | 10 | 0 | 11 | 0 |
| 3 Aug (last normal week) | 8 | 1 | 12 | 0 |
| **10 Aug — 4.2 ships** | **4** | **4** | 9 | 2 |
| 17 Aug | **0** | 2 | **14** | 2 |
| 24 Aug | **0** | 1 | **13** | 2 |
| 31 Aug | 0 | 0 | **15** | 2 |

Both took a missed-ping hit the week 4.2 shipped — this is the release
biting everyone, not a Farlight-specific problem. Nightwell's volume was
already high enough, and the following week strong enough, that the
penalty got absorbed and reversed. Farlight's wasn't, and it compounded
instead. Same rule, same release, opposite outcomes: this is what a
feedback loop with no floor looks like in practice, shown happening to
two named responders rather than argued in the abstract.

### It isn't just four responders — and location didn't stop mattering, it was overridden

Checking every area, not only the four worst-hit: home-turf dominance
(the share of an area's own callouts taken by that area's own responder)
dropped in 13 of 16 areas after 4.2. Three areas we hadn't otherwise
flagged — Riverside (Ironvale), Southport (Stormwrack), Kingsbridge (Sgt.
Falkirk) — lost 14 to 21 points of home-turf share each, even though all
three are growing in raw volume. Growth and erosion are happening to the
same responders at once: they're gaining work overall, partly by picking
up callouts elsewhere, while quietly ceding some of their own patch to
outsiders.

| Area (responder) | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uptown (Farlight) | 93.2% | 6.5% | −86.7 |
| Old Town (Vesper) | 97.2% | 13.2% | −84.0 |
| Harborside (The Undertow) | 92.1% | 9.4% | −82.7 |
| Eastgate (Meteor Mite) | 40.7% | 5.3% | −35.4 |
| Riverside (Ironvale) | 95.1% | 73.9% | −21.2 |
| Southport (Stormwrack) | 95.6% | 77.5% | −18.1 |
| Kingsbridge (Sgt. Falkirk) | 92.6% | 78.6% | −14.0 |
| 9 other areas | 89–98% | 87–95% | −2 to −8 (one area, Westbury, is +2.0) |

This raises an obvious question: is location simply mattering less now?
No — 4.2 moved `WEIGHT_PROXIMITY` **up** (0.45→0.60), specifically so
nearby responders would win more often. If the release worked as
intended, home-turf dominance should have gotten stronger, not weaker.
Instead it got weaker almost everywhere. That means the acceptance-score
bug is strong enough to override an *increased* proximity advantage, not
that proximity advantage stopped being the goal. In other words: the
feature responders asked for isn't delivering the benefit it was built
for, for exactly the people it should help most.

One limit on this: `availability.travel_time_minutes()` in the codebase
is a stub with no real travel-time data behind it, so this is a confident
read of the code's structure and the observed pattern, not a recomputed,
verified score. Worth asking Engineering directly whether the
acceptance term can mathematically cancel out any proximity advantage,
however large — if so, that's a design gap independent of the timeout
cut.

## Scale and confidence

- **4 responders** (Vesper, The Undertow, Meteor Mite, Farlight):
  confirmed in the pings data; 2 of them independently confirmed by
  support tickets. High confidence.
- **2 more** (Corporal Ashgrove, Halfmoon): real but milder — roughly a
  30% dip, one already recovering. Their tickets used the same urgent
  language as the severe cases; the severity is not the same and
  shouldn't be conflated.
- **2 responders raised in interviews with vivid anecdotes** (Captain
  Vantage, Sgt. Bulwark) are **not** actually declining — their weekly
  volumes are growing over the same period. Flagged explicitly so this
  doesn't get overcorrected based on a good story the data doesn't
  support.
- **Tickets lag the ping data by 5–11 days**, and missed 2 of the 4
  severe cases entirely — those handlers never filed. Pings are the
  leading indicator here; tickets confirm, they don't detect.

## What's still open, separate from this bug

- **Availability Confidence** was committed to 4.2 on the Q3 roadmap and
  never shipped, with no recorded deferral. This needs a Director-level
  answer, not an engineering one.
- **No year-ago baseline exists**, so seasonality can't be fully ruled
  out at the *aggregate* level — though the responder-level collapse,
  tied tightly to the release date, isn't explained by seasonality.
- **True time-to-accept** (the glossary's second official metric,
  alongside acceptance rate) isn't computable from the data we have —
  there's no accept timestamp, only ping-sent time and outcome. Would
  need to come from wherever Rook tracks that, if it exists anywhere.

## The ask

- **Engineering:** fix the missed-vs-declined scoring and add decay in
  `history.py`. Separable from whether the proximity/timeout tuning
  itself was right.
- **Product:** decide and record what happens to Availability
  Confidence; decide whether the proximity change stays as-is once the
  bug is fixed (likely yes — it was a genuine, long-standing responder
  ask).
- **Both:** agree on how to find and support the responders already in
  the hole — they won't recover on their own under the current scoring,
  however the fix is designed going forward.
